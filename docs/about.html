About Manjusha Guntur

Vedic astrology approached through curiosity, formal study, close observation, and an analytical mind.

I am Manjusha Guntur, a Vedic astrologer and technology architect. My relationship with astrology began, somewhat unexpectedly, with disbelief. For much of my life, I believed very strongly that almost anything could be achieved through effort, discipline, and complete commitment. If something mattered enough, my instinct was to work harder and give it everything I had. Over time, however, I encountered experiences in which I had done precisely that and still watched events unfold in ways that effort alone could not explain. It did not make me suddenly believe in destiny, but it did make me question whether my understanding of timing, circumstance, and human control was complete.

I have been an avid reader since childhood, so my natural response was to research. I did not begin reading astrology because I wanted to believe in it; I began because I wanted to understand it well enough to prove to myself that it was nonsense. I have never been comfortable dismissing an entire discipline with a blanket statement without first making the effort to understand what it actually says. Astrology deserved the same intellectual fairness.

As I read, I began comparing what I was learning with my own horoscope and with events from my own life, simply because that was the history I knew most intimately. What I expected to disprove became increasingly difficult to dismiss. Concepts that initially appeared abstract began to correspond with periods and experiences I could recognize. One book led to another, and after reading close to ten books, I knew that I had reached the limits of learning the subject entirely on my own.

By then, I wanted to learn Vedic astrology seriously, and if I was going to study formally, I wanted to learn from an accomplished guru rather than approach the subject casually. I had assumed that learning astrology would be relatively inexpensive, so discovering that serious instruction could cost thousands of dollars initially made me step away. The curiosity, however, did not disappear. Eventually, the desire to understand the discipline properly outweighed my hesitation, and in 2016 I began formal study with Dr. Dharmesh Mehta.

My professional life has unfolded in a very different world. I have spent more than fifteen years in software engineering, technical leadership, and architecture, working across distributed systems, Java, microservices, Kafka, cloud architecture, and AI native software engineering. Architecture has shaped the way I think. It teaches you to question the obvious explanation, examine relationships and dependencies, and understand that complex outcomes usually emerge from several influences operating together rather than from one factor viewed in isolation.

Long before I studied astrology, however, I was fascinated by people. From my teenage years, I was drawn to psychology books and to observing how people think, react, form attachments, make decisions, respond to pressure, and change with experience. I have always been interested in subjects that bring me closer to understanding human nature. Over time, I also became attentive to recurring patterns in behavior: what people repeat, what changes them, and why two people placed in seemingly similar circumstances can respond in entirely different ways.

That curiosity became unexpectedly valuable when I began studying astrology, because astrology itself is deeply concerned with patterns. A horoscope cannot be understood from one planet, one house, or one attractive combination. Sign and house placement, lordship, dignity, aspects, conjunctions, nakshatras, planetary periods, divisional charts, and transits continually qualify and reshape one another. What fascinates me is not merely identifying these individual factors, but understanding the larger pattern that emerges when the horoscope is read as an integrated whole and then observed across time.

The human dimension is equally important to me. A planetary period may be described technically through a chart, but it is lived by a person through ambition, uncertainty, relationships, responsibility, achievement, disappointment, faith, resilience, and change. My interest in psychology and years of observing people naturally influence the questions I ask of a horoscope. I want to understand not only what a period may indicate, but how a person experiences it, why different people respond differently to similar influences, and how time can alter perspective, priorities, and personality.

The Cosmic Pattern grew from that way of looking at astrology. I wanted to create a place where an ancient predictive discipline could be explored with intellectual seriousness, analytical depth, and sensitivity to the human being behind the horoscope. My writing examines planetary periods, timing, divisional charts, transits, predictive principles, and the broader patterns that become visible only when different layers of a chart are considered together.

My reason for writing, however, is much simpler than demonstrating knowledge or building a reputation. If something I write helps even one person understand a difficult period more clearly, eases some part of their pain or fear, offers perspective when life feels impossible to make sense of, or helps them move through that period with a little more steadiness, then the writing has fulfilled its purpose for me. Knowledge matters most when it becomes useful to another human being.

Alongside astrology, I continue to work as a Technical Lead and Solutions Architect and write about software architecture, AI native engineering, distributed systems, Java, microservices, Kafka, and cloud technologies. My professional projects and technology writing can be found across LinkedIn, GitHub, HackerNoon, Hashnode, DEV Community, and DZone.

I once approached astrology hoping that enough research would allow me to dismiss it. Instead, research gave me better questions, formal study gave those questions greater depth, and years of observing both horoscopes and people have continued to challenge what I think I understand. The Cosmic Pattern is where I explore those questions, with the same curiosity that started the journey in the first place.
